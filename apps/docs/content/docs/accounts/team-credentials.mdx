---
title: Shared credentials for a Team
description: Let a Team use a connected account, pool, provider connection or Saved Secret without copying it into everybody's setup.
---

One person connects the account and chooses who may use it. Delivery determines
who receives credential material and which controls can govern its use.

> **Availability:** Shared Team credentials are in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout, with [Teams](/accounts/teams). Stable 0.2 has neither — check the version of the server binary you are running.

<Callout>
  Cloud-provider delivery is an approved 0.3 development contract with integration
  still in progress. The description below explains its permissions and limits;
  it does not mean that a running build offers the complete journey. Explicit
  Action/source tests do not connect automatic installation at first launch or
  Session-end cleanup: those integrations are not wired yet. Loaded integration
  is parked, and real Vercel tenant validation is still outstanding.
</Callout>

## Availability

Shared credentials are a per-Home capability that depends on
[Teams](/accounts/teams). A self-hosted Home offers them unless its operator sets
`HAPPIER_FEATURE_TEAMS_CREDENTIAL_RESOURCES__ENABLED` to a false value. Where a
Home does not offer them you see **This Home does not offer shared credentials**
and personal connected accounts keep working exactly as before.

## What you need first

- Something to share: a **Connected account**, a **Connected service pool**, or a
  **Provider connection** with an external deployment that you own. See [Connected
  services](/accounts/connected-services) for setting those up.
- Ownership of that source. Only the person who owns a source can offer it.
- Owner or admin rights in the Team. **Shared credentials are managed by this
  Team's owners and admins.**

## Share a credential

Open **Settings → Teams**, choose the Team, then **Shared credentials** and
**Share credential**. Choose the source, name it, and decide who may use it.
For brokered Muse, choose your Muse **Connected account** and a broker machine
running the Happier daemon. If you use Muse through a personal **Managed
Gateway**, the Team share uses the Connected account behind that Gateway.

**Access and delivery** is where you choose the audience — **Everyone in the
Team**, particular **Groups**, particular **People**, or a mix. Access is
rechecked against current membership when Happier admits use. Removing somebody
withdraws their future entitlement. Delivery determines whether material or an
already-installed cloud rule can continue working outside that admission.

## Choose delivery with its disclosure in mind

The development contract gives the credential's owner an ordered list of allowed
delivery modes. New credentials allow only brokered delivery. Audience permission
is separate: allowing another mode never silently grants it to existing people
or Groups. A Team administrator cannot authorize disclosure of somebody else's key.

| Delivery | Where usable credential material goes | Happier request controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Through Happier broker | The source's broker machine sends upstream requests. | Current access, request policy and supported usage limits are checked for each brokered request. |
| Direct to machine | Local tools on the selected execution machine receive material. | Future delivery can be stopped; material already disclosed cannot be recalled or governed outside Happier. |
| Delivered by the machine's cloud provider | A qualified cloud provider receives the key and replaces a random placeholder outside its guest. Vercel is the first integration target. | Installation is admitted by Happier; subsequent native requests bypass its broker and per-request controls. |

Happier's Provider delivery owner chooses the owner's most-preferred permitted
mode supported by the selected machine and current readiness. Members select
their model normally, with no extra delivery choice or per-session override.
An unsupported combination needs an explanation or an explicitly permitted
alternative; it must not silently disclose a key directly.

Direct access is a real disclosure and Happier makes you say so: **Local tools
used by the people you choose may receive credential material for this source.
Removing access stops future delivery, but cannot erase material an external
process already used.** Treat it the way you would treat sending the key — because
in effect you are.

Services whose credentials cannot be exported can still be shared through the
broker where the service supports it. Muse sharing is brokered-only: members use
the Meta route through a compatible Agent such as Pi, OpenCode or Codex; the Muse
Agent itself uses a personal login. The credential owner uses their personal
connection rather than launching through their own brokered Team share.

### Cloud-provider delivery

The first development journey uses a company-controlled Vercel provisioning
connection and Team AI Gateway key, with the sandbox enrolled into the member's
Account. The provisioning token and Gateway key are separate credentials. An
ordinary approved Action installs the exact binding's rule; the Agent receives
its normal endpoint and placeholder after installation is confirmed. Declared
support, actual tenant qualification and current source authority are all needed.
Vercel [documents transformations on all plans, including Hobby](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/vercel-sandbox-vs-e2b);
that does not prove a particular tenant or installed sandbox is ready.

The key is intentionally disclosed to Vercel's control plane. An existing rule
may keep serving requests while the company controller is offline. New installs,
changes and cleanup need that control plane to be reachable. **Upstream key
rotation or revocation is the hard stop; controller-offline policy removal is
not guaranteed.** Partial or unknown updates are not confirmed installation or
removal. In 0.3 development, the next confirmed whole-policy replacement settles
earlier unknown outcomes and abandoned submission markers. Deleting a Session,
binding or shared credential schedules the current policy, including an empty
set, without keeping those records solely for cleanup. The controller reconciles
on reconnect and on the deletion notification; repeated unchanged policies do
not produce another vendor write.

Native traffic does not receive Happier's per-request membership checks, model/body
policy or request/token/cost enforcement. Vercel's request matchers narrow where
headers are substituted; unmatched requests are not blocked by those matchers.
Use verified Gateway or vendor budgets and key restrictions where available.
Session/member metadata is observational, not authenticated per-request identity
or an exact chargeback ledger.

Until separate member OS users and private execution material are implemented
and verified, members sharing a sandbox's Agent user can read each other's
placeholders and reuse their installed bindings. A filtered app response does
not supply OS privacy. Machine owners remain privileged, and a member's separately
granted Direct rights still allow disclosure. Native delivery is not currently
a route for external API keys, detached Runs or general shared Saved Secret uses.

### Model discovery in 0.3 development

For brokered Team Connected accounts and Pools, model discovery observes the
credential owner's selected source on the broker machine. Direct delivery uses
the execution machine's accepted source context and, after delivery, the running
Agent's model observation. An observed list replaces static declarations, even
when it is empty. With no observation, static fallback models show **Not checked**;
a ready route alone does not verify Account entitlement or successful inference.
See [Team model discovery](/agents/model-and-engine-selection#team-model-discovery-in-03-development)
for refresh and pre-launch limits in unreleased 0.3 development.

### Experimental model pairings

Experimental Team Agent/model pairings show an informational **Experimental**
badge. Neither the credential owner nor the recipient needs to confirm them;
they can be selected, tested and launched normally. An incompatible pairing
still refuses, and audience, disclosure, current-source and usage restrictions
still apply. **Not checked** describes missing catalog evidence separately.
Personal Provider connections keep their existing experimental confirmation.

### Direct delivery on a shared machine

The machine's owner can inspect keys and selected credential material delivered
to that machine for your session. This remains true with separate users for
members: the owner administers the machine and its daemon. It concerns material
you deliver there, rather than every credential in your Account.

In unreleased 0.3 development, the first isolation step uses one shared Agent
operating-system user, including for the owner's isolated Agent. Other members'
Agents can therefore read Direct-delivered keys and selected Session context on
that same machine. This step does not provide credential confidentiality between
Accounts. Separate Linux member users and private checkouts are being integrated
but are not yet verified end to end; an
enabled preference, pending setup or unknown readiness does not establish that
separation. Trust the machine's owner and the other people using its shared
Agent user before choosing Direct delivery. See [Sharing](/accounts/session-sharing)
for the shared-machine trust boundary.

When direct access is allowed, **Direct access readiness** shows who is actually
ready. Material is prepared on the computer that holds the source, whenever it is
online, and each recipient shows **Ready**, **Preparing access**, **Not delivered
yet**, **Waiting for encrypted-account setup**, or **Source changed — updating**.

**Check preparation** reads the current aggregate readiness through the same
Action available to session agents and MCP clients. It reports whether
preparation is complete and how many recipients are still pending;
it neither prepares material nor exposes keys, envelopes or ciphertext.

## Where the broker runs

A brokered credential needs a **Broker location**: the machine that holds the
source and makes the upstream request. Pick one machine, or pick a [Machine
Pool](/apps/daemon#machine-pools) and let the Home choose an available member
from the first eligible tier for each new broker connection. Source-owning
machines report whether the credential source is usable there without returning
the credential or its settings.

Deleting the selected Machine Pool clears the credential's broker location. Choose
a new Machine or Machine Pool before brokered use can continue. The credential and
its keys stay configured; Happier does not silently choose a replacement location.

If the broker is offline you see **The broker machine for this credential is not
reachable right now**. The credential and its keys stay configured — bring the
machine back or choose another broker location rather than recreating anything.

If your Session briefly disconnects while using a brokered credential, retry
the turn after the Session reconnects.

In 0.3 development, if Happier says the shared credential connection is not
ready on the machine running your Session, check that machine's connection to
your Home before retrying the Session.

## Limits

**Limits** cap what the Team may spend through one shared credential. Each limit
names three things that cannot be changed afterwards — **Applies to** (the entire
shared credential, each member separately, one Group, or one person), **Measure**
(requests, tokens, or cost), and **Period** (daily, weekly, or monthly) — plus a
**Maximum** you can edit. To change the identity of a limit, remove it and add a
new one.

New requests stop once recorded usage reaches the limit; requests already running
may finish, so a period can overshoot slightly. A limit on a Group is shared by
everybody in it. A cost limit needs a known price for every model the credential
allows, and Happier refuses to create one when a price is missing rather than
quietly treating it as zero — limit requests or tokens instead.

Editing a limit does not end a running Session. Its next brokered request is
checked against the updated limit and refused if recorded usage has reached it.
Requests already admitted may finish.

**Limits cover supported brokered and external API use.** Direct and cloud-provider
delivery bypass broker admission, so Happier cannot enforce these limits against
their requests. An Agent may still report token or cost observations; those do
not establish complete request counts or enforceable native quotas. Usage coverage
must remain explicit rather than presenting unobserved spend as zero.

## What gets recorded

**Usage** shows recorded requests for the selected period, and can break the total
down by person, external API key, model, session, source account, worker machine,
broker machine, or delivery mode. Token and cost totals depend on what the
provider protocol reports, so Happier marks a period as incomplete rather than
rounding missing measurements down to zero. **Export CSV** saves the period.
The CSV marks labels that begin with spreadsheet formula characters as text.

**Activity** records the administrative changes — who shared the credential,
changed its settings, changed who can use it, delivered direct access, created or
revoked an external API key, changed limits, or deleted it. It does not record
prompts, responses, request bodies, or secret values.

## Request policy

**Request policy** narrows what the credential may be asked to do: which request
formats (OpenAI Responses, OpenAI Chat Completions, Anthropic Messages), which
models, and which reasoning effort. Token ceilings stay with the request itself —
Happier does not rewrite the output or thinking budget a session asks for. A
session that is already running is not rewritten — its next brokered request has
to meet the new policy. Direct and cloud-provider traffic do not pass through
this request-policy enforcement.

In 0.3 development, if the Agent cannot use any reasoning effort that the Team
allows for your chosen model, starting a brokered session shows you both sets of
efforts. Choose an Agent or model that supports one of the allowed levels, or
ask a Team manager to review the request policy.

## Using a shared credential in a session

When you pick a shared credential for a session, **Where members can use it**
decides the context it is allowed in: **Any permitted session**, **Sessions whose
Team is this one**, or **Sessions this Team can see**. The stricter choices can
mean that selecting the credential shares an otherwise private session with the
Team — Happier asks first, with **Share this session with the Team?**, and never
does it silently.

If a shared credential is deleted, new Happier admission fails. Its next brokered
request is refused. Material already delivered directly cannot be recalled,
and cloud-provider removal remains pending or unconfirmed until the vendor change
is established; revoke or rotate the upstream key when independent withdrawal
is needed.

In 0.3 development, a dedicated Voice agent cannot follow a session's Team
credential model. Voice reports a setup error before starting instead of using
personal credentials for the same model. Choose an explicit supported Voice
model, or use [Direct to session](/voice/local-providers#direct-to-session) to
send speech into the Team-backed session.

An unexpected broker application fault returns a typed `broker_unavailable`
response with HTTP 503, so clients can distinguish temporary unavailability
from an access or policy refusal (HTTP 403). The shared credential remains
selected; Happier does not switch to a personal connection.

## Shared Saved Secrets

A Saved Secret — an API key, token, or password you have stored in Happier — can
be shared with people, Teams, and Groups too. In **Settings → Secrets**, use **Add
a shared secret** to create one for sharing (it is not added to your personal
secrets), or **Manage access** on one you already own.

A shared Saved Secret stays one resource rather than becoming a copy in each
recipient's account, and the grant is rechecked at use time. Sharing one is a
disclosure in the same sense direct credential access is: **People you share with
can use this secret anywhere Happier accepts Saved Secrets. Apps and local tools
may receive its value.**

Each recipient row carries its own material status — **Ready**, **Preparing
encrypted access**, **Unavailable for this account encryption mode**,
**Temporarily unavailable**, **Access removed**, **Deleted**, or **Update Happier
to use this shared secret** — so an unusable secret is visibly unusable instead of
failing at the moment somebody needs it.

### Change how a shared secret is stored

A shared Saved Secret is stored either end-to-end encrypted or **Home-managed**
(the Home can read it). It starts in your account's mode, and you can change it
later from the secret's menu in **Settings → Secrets**. The secret keeps its
identity, so everything that references it and everyone you shared it with stay
in place.

- **Make Home-managed** decrypts the value on your device and hands it to the
  Home. Happier asks you to confirm first, because the value stops being
  end-to-end encrypted. Use this when you want to share with someone whose
  account is Home-managed: they cannot open an end-to-end encrypted secret.
- **End-to-end encrypt** re-encrypts the value on your device with a new key.
  Recipients with end-to-end encrypted accounts keep access. Recipients whose
  accounts are Home-managed keep their grant, but they see **Unavailable for
  this account encryption mode** until you make the secret Home-managed again.

Each option appears only where it can work. **End-to-end encrypt** needs an end-to-end encrypted
account. **Make Home-managed** needs a Home whose storage policy allows
Home-managed content. A Home that requires end-to-end encryption refuses it.
Changing a secret's storage never changes anyone's account mode.
