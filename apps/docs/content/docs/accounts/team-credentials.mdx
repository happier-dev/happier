---
title: Shared credentials for a Team
description: Let a Team use a connected account, pool, provider connection or Saved Secret without copying it into everybody's setup.
---

One person connects the account. Everybody in the Team can use it, under limits
you set, without ever holding the credential themselves. That is what a shared
credential is for — and it is the difference between onboarding somebody in a
minute and mailing them an API key.

> **Availability:** Shared Team credentials are in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout, with [Teams](/accounts/teams). Stable 0.2 has neither — check the version of the server binary you are running.

## Availability

Shared credentials are a per-Home capability that depends on
[Teams](/accounts/teams). A self-hosted Home offers them unless its operator sets
`HAPPIER_FEATURE_TEAMS_CREDENTIAL_RESOURCES__ENABLED` to a false value. Where a
Home does not offer them you see **This Home does not offer shared credentials**
and personal connected accounts keep working exactly as before.

## What you need first

- Something to share: a **Connected account**, a **Connected service pool**, or a
  **Provider connection** you own. See [Connected
  services](/accounts/connected-services) for setting those up.
- Ownership of that source. Only the person who owns a source can offer it.
- Owner or admin rights in the Team. **Shared credentials are managed by this
  Team's owners and admins.**

## Share a credential

Open **Settings → Teams**, choose the Team, then **Shared credentials** and
**Share credential**. Choose the source, name it, and decide who may use it.

**Access and delivery** is where you choose the audience — **Everyone in the
Team**, particular **Groups**, particular **People**, or a mix. Access is
rechecked at use time against current membership, so removing somebody from the
Team or a Group takes their access away without you editing the credential.

## Brokered and direct, and why the difference matters

Every shared credential has a **Direct disclosure** ceiling, on the **Edit**
screen:

- **Brokered only** (the safe default) — the credential material never leaves the
  machine that holds it. When somebody in the Team makes a provider request,
  Happier routes it through that broker machine, which injects the credential
  there. Recipients get results, not secrets.
- **Allow direct access** — Happier may hand usable credential material to local
  tools on the recipient's own machine, which some agents need in order to talk to
  a provider themselves.

Direct access is a real disclosure and Happier makes you say so: **Local tools
used by the people you choose may receive credential material for this source.
Removing access stops future delivery, but cannot erase material an external
process already used.** Treat it the way you would treat sending the key — because
in effect you are.

When direct access is allowed, **Direct access readiness** shows who is actually
ready. Material is prepared on the computer that holds the source, whenever it is
online, and each recipient shows **Ready**, **Preparing access**, **Not delivered
yet**, **Waiting for encrypted-account setup**, or **Source changed — updating**.

**Check preparation** reads the current aggregate readiness through the same
Action available to session agents and MCP clients. It reports whether
preparation is complete and how many recipients are ready or still pending;
it neither prepares material nor exposes keys, envelopes or ciphertext.

## Where the broker runs

A brokered credential needs a **Broker location**: the machine that holds the
source and makes the upstream request. Pick one machine, or pick a [Machine
Pool](/apps/daemon#machine-pools) and let the Home choose an available member
from the first eligible tier for each new broker connection. Source-owning
machines report whether the credential source is usable there without returning
the credential or its settings.

Deleting the selected Machine Pool clears the credential's broker location. Choose
a new Machine or Machine Pool before brokered use can continue. The credential and
its keys stay configured; Happier does not silently choose a replacement location.

If the broker is offline you see **The broker machine for this credential is not
reachable right now**. The credential and its keys stay configured — bring the
machine back or choose another broker location rather than recreating anything.

## Limits

**Limits** cap what the Team may spend through one shared credential. Each limit
names three things that cannot be changed afterwards — **Applies to** (the entire
shared credential, each member separately, one Group, or one person), **Measure**
(requests, tokens, or cost), and **Period** (daily, weekly, or monthly) — plus a
**Maximum** you can edit. To change the identity of a limit, remove it and add a
new one.

New requests stop once recorded usage reaches the limit; requests already running
may finish, so a period can overshoot slightly. A limit on a Group is shared by
everybody in it. A cost limit needs a known price for every model the credential
allows, and Happier refuses to create one when a price is missing rather than
quietly treating it as zero — limit requests or tokens instead.

**Limits cover brokered and external API use.** Direct use happens on the
recipient's own machine, so Happier records none of it and no limit can be
enforced against it. Where everybody with access uses a credential directly, the
limits screen says so plainly rather than displaying a number that means nothing.

## What gets recorded

**Usage** shows recorded requests for the selected period, and can break the total
down by person, external API key, model, session, source account, worker machine,
broker machine, or delivery mode. Token and cost totals depend on what the
provider protocol reports, so Happier marks a period as incomplete rather than
rounding missing measurements down to zero. **Export CSV** saves the period.

**Activity** records the administrative changes — who shared the credential,
changed its settings, changed who can use it, delivered direct access, created or
revoked an external API key, changed limits, or deleted it. It does not record
prompts, responses, request bodies, or secret values.

## Request policy

**Request policy** narrows what the credential may be asked to do: which request
formats (OpenAI Responses, OpenAI Chat Completions, Anthropic Messages), which
models, and which reasoning effort. Token ceilings stay with the request itself —
Happier does not rewrite the output or thinking budget a session asks for. A
session that is already running is not rewritten — its next request has to meet
the new policy.

## Using a shared credential in a session

When you pick a shared credential for a session, **Where members can use it**
decides the context it is allowed in: **Any permitted session**, **Sessions whose
Team is this one**, or **Sessions this Team can see**. The stricter choices can
mean that selecting the credential shares an otherwise private session with the
Team — Happier asks first, with **Share this session with the Team?**, and never
does it silently.

If a shared credential is deleted, members lose access immediately and the next
request fails. Material already delivered directly cannot be recalled.

## Shared Saved Secrets

A Saved Secret — an API key, token, or password you have stored in Happier — can
be shared with people, Teams, and Groups too. In **Settings → Secrets**, use **Add
a shared secret** to create one for sharing (it is not added to your personal
secrets), or **Manage access** on one you already own.

A shared Saved Secret stays one resource rather than becoming a copy in each
recipient's account, and the grant is rechecked at use time. Sharing one is a
disclosure in the same sense direct credential access is: **People you share with
can use this secret anywhere Happier accepts Saved Secrets. Apps and local tools
may receive its value.**

Each recipient row carries its own material status — **Ready**, **Preparing
encrypted access**, **Unavailable for this account encryption mode**,
**Temporarily unavailable**, **Access removed**, **Deleted**, or **Update Happier
to use this shared secret** — so an unusable secret is visibly unusable instead of
failing at the moment somebody needs it.

### Change how a shared secret is stored

A shared Saved Secret is stored either end-to-end encrypted or **Home-managed**
(the Home can read it). It starts in your account's mode, and you can change it
later from the secret's menu in **Settings → Secrets**. The secret keeps its
identity, so everything that references it and everyone you shared it with stay
in place.

- **Make Home-managed** decrypts the value on your device and hands it to the
  Home. Happier asks you to confirm first, because the value stops being
  end-to-end encrypted. Use this when you want to share with someone whose
  account is Home-managed: they cannot open an end-to-end encrypted secret.
- **End-to-end encrypt** re-encrypts the value on your device with a new key.
  Recipients with end-to-end encrypted accounts keep access. Recipients whose
  accounts are Home-managed keep their grant, but they see **Unavailable for
  this account encryption mode** until you make the secret Home-managed again.

Each option appears only where it can work. **End-to-end encrypt** needs an end-to-end encrypted
account. **Make Home-managed** needs a Home whose storage policy allows
Home-managed content. A Home that requires end-to-end encryption refuses it.
Changing a secret's storage never changes anyone's account mode.
