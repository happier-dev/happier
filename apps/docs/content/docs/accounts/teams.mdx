title: Teams
description: Create a Team on one Home, give people roles, group them, and decide what new sessions do by default.
---

A Team is one shared place on one Home for sessions, people, and access. Create
one and you can share a session with everybody in it instead of naming people one
at a time, hand out shared credentials once, and set what new sessions do by
default for the whole group.

> **Availability:** Teams are in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout. Stable 0.2 has no Teams — check the version of the server binary you are running.

## Availability

Teams are a per-Home capability, offered unless the Home's operator turns them
off — on a self-hosted Home, with `HAPPIER_FEATURE_TEAMS__ENABLED`. A client that
reaches a Home without Teams shows **Teams are not available on this Home**
rather than half a feature, and everything else on that Home keeps working.

A Team belongs to the Home it was created on and stays there. If you use more
than one Home, each one has its own Teams, and the Teams list tells you which
Home each Team came from. When a Home stops being reachable, the Teams it has
already shown you stay in the list, marked **Showing the last known data for this
Home** — losing them would throw away real information. It is a Home whose Teams
have never loaded on this device that contributes no rows; Happier names that Home
as unavailable instead of pretending the list is complete.

## Request attribution in 0.3 development

A shared Bot keeps the cause of a request separate from the Session that answers
and the Account whose credentials execute it. Read these facts as **asked by**,
**answered by** and **using**. Delegating an answer does not turn the answering
Session into the original requester. These are development data contracts;
the corresponding bylines are still being built.

Attribution comes from authenticated host evidence. Unknown or autonomous
requests stay unknown or autonomous; Happier does not infer a person from a
Bot's owner or a name in message text. Older history may lack those facts.
It is not a complete audit log or an access grant.

For an outward write, the original Bot's setup and credentials execute after
the [requester's confirmation](/sessions/inbox-and-approvals#shared-bot-confirmation-in-03-development).
Team membership alone does not supply that consent or make the Bot ready;
see [Bot readiness](/organize/session-organization#bot-readiness-in-03-development).

### Shared machines and credentials

Team membership, Machine access and Session sharing are separate grants. A Team
owner or admin is not automatically the owner of a Machine; that owner is its
enrolled Account on the Machine's Home. Machine **Can manage** includes trusted
administration and can defeat operating-system isolation.

In unreleased 0.3 development, Machine **Can use** also permits Workflows and
schedules. Use your own Connected Account credentials and review the
[sign-in lifetime and private Run boundaries](/accounts/session-sharing).

Shared-machine execution is incomplete, unreleased 0.3 development work. Its
first isolation step uses one shared Agent operating-system user for members and
the owner's isolated Agent. Selected credentials, Direct-delivered keys and
readable Session context remain visible to peers sharing that user; it does not
establish confidentiality between Accounts. The Machine owner can inspect
material delivered there even when members have separate users. Separate Linux
users and private checkouts are now being integrated, not yet verified as a
complete usable journey. See
[shared-machine trust](/accounts/session-sharing) and
[Direct delivery](/accounts/team-credentials#direct-delivery-on-a-shared-machine)
before choosing a shared target.

### Team pools and presets in 0.3 development

An administrator can make a Machine Pool available to a Team from the existing
Pool editor. The Pool still belongs to its custodian Account. Team members see its
public details without its controller list or permission to run directly on those
controllers.

When editing a Team Machine preset, choose a Pool as the controller location and
enable **Members may create**. Members can then choose that preset in **New
Session**. Creating the Machine chooses one eligible, connected Pool controller
and enrolls a fresh container or VM to the requesting member's Account. The
preset keeps its creation cap and retention policy.

Pool placement is available for provisioners that create a guest through a
Machine's daemon. Account-hosted provisioners keep their existing creation flow.
The review shows one recipe common to the Pool's enabled, compatible controllers,
including any that are offline. If their declared recipes differ, the preset is
unavailable until the Pool or preset is updated. Only connected controllers can
be selected for creation. A prerequisite
that has not been observed is shown as unknown; an executor can refuse creation
if it is missing. If no eligible controller is available, Happier reports that
creation is unavailable. It does not wait for capacity or silently try another
controller after creation starts. Removing a member or withdrawing delegation
prevents their next creation request. If a Pool or preset controller's custodian
leaves the Team or loses active membership, remaining members cannot start new
delegated creations using that custodian's compute or Connected Accounts. Their
published Pool becomes unavailable to the Team. Work already admitted keeps its
existing lifecycle, and the custodian retains their own compute access.

These controls are unreleased 0.3 development features. See
[Machine Pools](/apps/daemon#machine-pools) for availability and exact-target behavior.

## Create a Team

Open **Settings → Teams** and choose **New Team**. Give it a **Name** and, if it
helps, a **Description**. The Team is created on the Home you are looking at, and
you become its first owner.

Two Teams may share a name. Names are labels: invitation links and access always
refer to the Team itself, never to its name.

Some Homes administer Team creation centrally. There you see **Team creation is
administered on this Home** and an administrator creates the Team and chooses its
**First owner**. Creating a Team for somebody else does not add you to it.

## Roles

Every membership carries exactly one role, and the role decides what the person
gets by default:

| Role | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Owns the Team, may manage it, and may change owners. |
| **Admin** | Receives Member access and may manage the Team. |
| **Member** | Receives access granted to the Team by default. |
| **Guest** | Sees only Sessions and resources explicitly shared with this Account or with a Group it belongs to. |

Guest is the role to reach for when somebody should collaborate on a few named
sessions without seeing the Team's shared work. The invitation screen says so
before they accept: joining as Guest does not give access to that Team's Team
Sessions.

A Team always keeps at least one active owner. If you try to remove, suspend, or
demote the last one, Happier stops you with **A Team keeps at least one active
owner** and asks you to choose another owner first.

## Members

A Team's page opens with what is going on in it: its shared sessions, how many
people it has (and how many are suspended), its Groups and the directory that keeps
them in step, the invitations still waiting, how members sign in, the credentials it
shares, and its sharing and history defaults. Trouble — a directory that couldn't
sync, an invitation email that didn't arrive — appears first under **Needs your
attention**, each with a way to deal with it. A Team you have just created shows its first steps
instead — invite people, choose how members sign in, share a session — until
someone else joins or is invited. **Invite people** at the top of the page opens
the invitation for that Team.

In unreleased 0.3 development source, the overview also lists Workflows shared
with this Team. Open a Workflow and choose **History** on a Team-visible
schedule row to see History filtered to that trigger, with **Edited by**
attribution on its occurrences. The owner's private Runs stay excluded; a
personal Workflow share alone does not expose their history. See
[Workflow Team visibility](/extras/automations#team-visibility).

The **Members** tab lists everybody in the Team with their role and status, and
filters for owners and admins, members, guests, and suspended people. Opening a
person keeps the Members list beside them on a wide screen, so you can move from one
person to the next. A member without management rights sees the roster and each
person's role, and is told who can change it. **Add
member** offers two ways in: **Add someone on this Home** for a person who already
has an Account here, or **Invite by link or email** for anybody else (see
[invitations](/accounts/team-invitations)). If you can do only one of the two, the
button goes straight there.

Three lifecycle actions, each with a different blast radius:

- **Suspend member** stops Team and Group access immediately while keeping the
  person's Group membership and resource assignments. Reactivating restores only
  the access that is still valid. Their Home Account and their other Teams are
  untouched.
- **Reactivate member** resumes access where memberships, Groups, and Account
  state still allow it.
- **Remove from Team** ends current Team and Group access and removes Group
  memberships and membership-lifetime grants. It does not erase prior authorship
  or content the person already saw. Rejoining later starts a new membership.

Removing someone asks once, in plain sentences: their access ends, the Groups they
leave by name, and that what they wrote stays.

### Leave a Team

Choose **Leave Team** on your own entry in **Members**. If you do not manage the
Team, the same action is at the bottom of its overview. Confirming ends your
Team and Group access and removes your Group memberships and grants tied to that
membership. Your Home Account, prior authorship, and content you already saw stay.
You return to the Teams list, where that Team no longer appears. Rejoining later
starts a new membership.

A directory-managed membership must be changed at its source. The final active
owner of a live Team must hand ownership over or archive the Team before leaving.
Leave follows the same approval policy as removing a member; an agent can request
it through the session Agent or CLI surface when you ask. Both operations appear
in the Home’s administration activity, with your own Account as actor and subject
when you leave.

Where a membership comes from a directory, the row names that source and the role
is read-only here — change it in the source. You can move a membership between
Happier and a directory with **Managed by** on the person's page; that
keeps the membership, its role, status, and session history, and changes only who
may edit it.

### Encrypted access, and what Prepare does

If your Home stores sessions with end-to-end encryption, a new member's Account
cannot read existing Team session history until somebody who can read it prepares
that access for them. The member's detail screen shows this under **Encrypted
access**:

- **Ready** — nothing to do. They can open the Team's encrypted sessions.
- **Needs preparing** — the sessions exist, the member cannot read them yet.
  Choose **Prepare encrypted access** and Happier re-wraps each session's key for
  that member's Account from your device, showing progress as it goes.
- **Setup required** — the person has not finished setting up encrypted access on
  their own Account. Prepare becomes available once they have.
- **Not encrypted** — their Account does not use end-to-end encryption, so there
  is nothing to prepare.

Preparing runs from your device, using your own access, so it can only cover
sessions you can currently read. If some cannot be prepared from this device you
see **Encrypted access needs repair**: open those sessions once to repair your own
access, then prepare again. Sessions created in an older encryption format cannot
be shared with new members at all; they stay readable for the people who already
have them. If preparation stops partway, whatever was already prepared is kept —
run it again to finish.

## What an agent can request

In the unreleased 0.3 checkout, a session agent or MCP client can request an
invitation, reissue an invitation, test a shared credential, or preview and
remove a directory source. Each call requires approval from a present user;
turning off optional approval prompts does not waive that requirement.

Invitation results shown to agents contain safe invitation details, never the
bearer link. The approving user's UI shows the one-time link for copying,
sharing or scanning. **Check preparation** is a separate read: agents and the
UI receive readiness and the pending-recipient count, not credential material or recipient
encryption keys. Preparing the material still runs on the source's computer.

## Groups

A Group is a flat set of Team members you can share with together: **Developers**,
**Design**, **On-call**. Create one from the **Groups** tab, add members, and from
then on you can grant a session or a credential to the Group instead of to each
person.

Groups do not nest and do not carry roles of their own. A person's role comes from
their Team membership; a Group only decides what gets shared with them as a set.

Archiving a Group stops Group-based access immediately while keeping its
membership and history, so restoring it can make those grants effective again. A
Group fed by a directory shows **Managed by** that source: its name and lifecycle
belong to the source, though you can still add people to it in Happier.

## What new sessions do by default

The **Settings** tab holds the decisions that apply to the whole Team.

Under **Sharing**, each choice applies as soon as you pick it, and the line under
it says what the current choice means. Nothing already shared changes.

**Session defaults** decides what happens to a session somebody starts in this
Team:

- **Private** — nothing is shared until the person shares it.
- **Shared** — new sessions start visible to the Team, and the person can still
  make one private.
- **Always shared** — new sessions are shared and stay that way.

This applies to new sessions only. Existing private sessions are never exposed by
changing it.

**External sharing** decides who may share a Team session outside the Team —
**Anyone** who can share it, **Admins** only, or **Nobody**. It blocks future
sharing; it cannot withdraw a copy already shared.

**History default** preselects the **Session history** choice offered when
somebody joins: **From joining** (only sessions shared after they join) or
**Earlier too** (sessions already shared with the Team as well). It is a default
for new members, not a rewrite of the history existing members already have. The
choice works at whole-session granularity — it does not reveal only the messages
created after somebody joined.

The Team's name, description and logo are saved together: **Cancel** and **Save**
appear beside the section title while you have changes. People who cannot change
these settings see the current values and who can change them.

A **Logo** replaces the Team's monogram everywhere the Team appears. Team names
and descriptions, Group membership, and policy are administration data the Home
can read, including on an end-to-end encrypted Account, so keep secrets out of them.

Group names and descriptions follow the author's Account mode: end-to-end encrypted
Accounts seal them for the Team's recipients; Plain Accounts keep them readable to
the Home. Older Group text remains readable until its next ordinary metadata save.
A Group name supplied by an external directory remains visible to the Home in that
upstream directory record.

### Managed Session preferences (0.3 development)

Unreleased development source also lets administrators supply Session preferences
under **Defaults for members**: in the Team's **Settings** for everyone, and on a
Group's page for that Group. Each row shows the current default or **None**, and
people who can't change them see why. Agents can set the same values through the
existing Team policy action. The first New Session consumer preselects
the permission mode for the selected Agent. Placement, connected-service and model
preferences have a shared data contract; their consumers are still being integrated.

Your explicit choice takes priority over Group and Team preferences. An agreeing
Group preference comes before the Team preference; conflicting Groups fall back
to the Team. These preferences do not change your Account settings or grant access
to a Machine, model or credential.

Only a locked [organization policy](/self-hosting/organization-policy) fixes the
permission value and shows **Managed by** the organization. An unlocked policy
supplies a fallback below personal, Group and Team choices. The policy file is
local to the desktop machine; browser and mobile clients do not read it.

## Archiving a Team

**Archive** moves a Team out of active views. Team- and Group-based access stops
and outstanding invitation links are revoked, while memberships, Groups, policies,
and existing grants are retained — restoring the Team may make those retained
grants effective again. Revoked invitation links do not come back; issue new ones.
Archived Teams stay listed under **Show archived** and are read-only until
restored; the Team's **Settings** page says it is archived and offers **Restore**.

## Team sign-in

A Team can require a particular sign-in — your company IdP rather than an email
and password — and can receive its membership from a directory instead of from
invitations. That lives on the **Authentication** tab and is set up together with
the Home operator; see [Enterprise
identity](/self-hosting/auth-enterprise-identity) for the whole model, including
the Team's shareable member sign-in page, WorkOS, GitHub organizations, and
directory sync.

Signing in to a Home never joins a Team by itself. Admission is a separate
decision the Team's policy makes.

## When it goes wrong

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Teams are not available on this Home** | The Home has Teams turned off, needs an update, or is unreachable. |
| **Showing the last known data for this Home** | You are looking at cached state because the Home is not reachable right now. |
| **Someone else changed this first** | Another manager saved first. Review the current values, then apply your change again. |
| **You do not have permission for this change** | Your role does not allow it. Owners and admins manage the Team. |
| **This Team is archived** | Restore it before making changes. |
| **The Home may have completed this change** | The result was lost in transit. Refresh the Team before retrying, rather than repeating the change blind. |
