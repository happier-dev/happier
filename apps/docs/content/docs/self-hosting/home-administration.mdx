---
title: Home administration
description: Manage people, sign-in, reachability, mail and server settings for a Home you administer.
---

Open a Home's console to manage its people and Teams, decide who can sign in,
configure mail and addresses, and review settings that need a server restart.

> **Availability:** Home Administration is in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout. It is not evidence that an installed stable or preview server already offers it — check the version of the server binary you are running.

## What you need first

- An account on that Home holding the **Owner** or **Admin** role. An owner is
  named once, from the machine running the server — see
  [Home owner setup and recovery](/self-hosting/home-owner). Signing up never makes
  anyone an owner.
- The Home selected in the app. Administration is always of one named Home; each
  Home keeps its own accounts, roles and policies.

Open **Settings → Homes**, choose the Home, then open **Home administration**.
The console names the Home and your role. Its navigation sits beside the page on
wide screens and becomes a menu in narrower windows; phones open each page separately.

**Overview** lists only what needs you — a value the server ignored at its last
start, mail or a public address that is missing, a sign-in platform that is half
set up, changes waiting for a restart — each with the page that fixes it. Values
your deployment fixes are not listed there; each row that has one says so. An owner
renames the Home from **This Home → Home name**; the name is the one devices show.

## Roles

Three roles, and the third one is not an administrator at all:

| Role | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Administer everything on this Home, including deleting accounts and changing owners. |
| **Admin** | Administer accounts and Teams, but not change owners. |
| **Member** | No Home administration authority. |

Owners change authentication policy, Home settings and runtime configuration.
Admins read those facts and use the account and Team actions their capabilities
allow. Reading sign-in deployment facts does not grant access to the owner-only
managed-provider lists or their mutation controls.

A Home always needs at least one **active** owner. Any change that would remove the
last one is refused with *This Home needs at least one active owner. Make another
account an owner first.* — that includes demoting yourself, disabling the last
owner, and deleting them. Holding a role and being able to use it are different
things: putting an account on hold suspends its authority but keeps its assigned
role, re-enabling the account restores that authority, and the only way to take a
role away is to demote the account explicitly.

Home roles are governance only. Being an owner does not give you Team membership,
access to anyone's Sessions, or any encryption key material. Recovering ownership
of a Home does not make its end-to-end encrypted content readable, and it never
will: the server does not hold those keys.

## People

The **People** tab lists every account on the Home, each with a role and a
lifecycle state:

- **Active** — signed in and usable.
- **Disabled** — *Signed out everywhere. Can be re-enabled.*
- **Retired** — *Access permanently revoked.* There is no way back.

Search resolves accounts by name on Homes that offer it; older Homes report that
search is unavailable rather than silently returning nothing.

Open a person to see their sign-in email and methods, linked identity providers,
Teams and roles, Machine and API-token counts, and recent administration events.
These are administration facts; token contents and encryption keys are not shown.
**Sign out everywhere** ends the person's device sign-ins without deleting their
account. Device sessions are not tracked individually; API tokens are separate.

A failed refresh keeps the last-known detail with a visible error and **Retry**.
If the Home says the account is gone or you can no longer read it, that answer
replaces the detail and its actions.

### Disable and re-enable

**Disable account** is the reversible hold, and it is not a gentle one. The
confirmation states what goes:

> They will be signed out on every device and their machines will disconnect.
> Personal access tokens are permanently revoked, Session responsibility is cleared,
> and on every Session they lose access to their unsent drafts are discarded and
> their Follow is removed. Re-enabling restores access but not those drafts, Follow
> or responsibility. Team membership and encryption keys are kept.

**Re-enable account** lets them sign in again on their devices. Tokens revoked by
the disable stay revoked — re-enabling is not un-revoking.

Neither state stores a reason or a timestamp, and Happier does not invent one.

### Delete

**Delete account and data…** permanently erases the account and its data on that
Home. It cannot be undone, and any Home or Team ownership has to be transferred
first — a Team that still needs this account as its owner blocks the deletion with
*A Team still needs this account as its owner. Give that Team another owner first.*

If cleanup does not finish, Happier says so instead of pretending it did: access is
revoked and the account is **Retired**, and **Retry deletion** completes the rest. A
member who sees this state is told a Home owner or the server operator can finish
it.

## Policies

**Policies** contains sign-in, admission and encryption. Owners change
authentication policy. Saving checks the revision you loaded, so two administrators
editing at once get *This policy changed elsewhere* rather than a silent overwrite.

### Team creation

Who can create Teams is set at the top of the console's **Teams** page, where
admins can change it when authorised:

- **Anyone** — active members create a Team and become its owner.
- **Admins** — owners and admins create Teams and choose the initial owner.
- **Nobody** — no new Teams; existing Teams are unchanged.

### Sign-in and admission

By default a Home uses the server's defaults. An owner can turn each of these on
or off from here, in either direction, unless the server's environment sets it
explicitly: then the row shows **Fixed by your deployment** with the setting's
name, and only the operator can change it.

- **Sign-in methods** — which methods this Home offers. A method that needs
  something first says so: GitHub sign-in without a GitHub sign-in app shows
  *Needs a GitHub sign-in app first* with **Set up**, which opens that app's row on
  **Sign-in providers**. Only something the deployment alone can provide is named
  by its environment key.
- **Account types** — **Plain**, **End-to-end encrypted**, or both, plus which is
  **Recommended for new accounts**. Changing the recommendation sets the default for
  new accounts and changes no existing one.
- **Who can join** — **Self-service**, **Invitation only**, or **Closed**, and
  **Anonymous sign-up** (an account with only a recovery key, no email).
- **Storage policy** — whether accounts must use end-to-end encryption. It applies
  when the server next starts; until then the page says **Applies after restart**.

Nobody loses their last way to sign in: a change that would leave any account
without one is refused. Changes apply to new accounts and sign-ins only; existing
accounts keep their encryption.

Turning something **on** asks you to confirm first, because it lets more people
in or protects data less: the confirmation says who could now sign up and that the
change is recorded in **Activity** with your name. Turning something off saves
straight away.
- **Sign-in service** — a Home whose deployment offers one can turn it off here
  (*The Home sign-in service is turned off.*). It cannot point the Home at a
  different service; that is the operator's environment configuration, described in
  [Authentication](/self-hosting/auth).

If a Home stores a sign-in configuration this server version cannot read, Happier
says **Configuration needs attention** and sign-in stays unavailable until the
operator repairs it. It does not guess a safe interpretation.

## Sign-in providers

Use **Sign-in providers** for managed identity providers, GitHub Apps, private
identity endpoints and Team sign-in rules. Deployment-defined OIDC providers
remain read-only and are identified separately from managed instances.

**Sign-in platforms**, at the top of the page, holds the credentials the Home
signs in through: **GitHub sign-in** (the GitHub OAuth app and its callback
address, **Who can sign in with GitHub**, and the rarely changed keys under
**Advanced**) and **WorkOS** (client ID and API key).
Closed, each row says its state in one line — not set up, what it still needs,
saved and waiting for a restart, ready, set by your deployment, or not in use
because the last start could not read a value. Open a row to edit it in place:
nothing is written until you press the row's **Save**, which saves its fields and
secrets together. A key explicitly set in the server environment stays locked,
key by key, so a deployment-set API key and an owner-set client ID can sit side
by side. A secret that is not set yet is an open masked field; once saved it shows
presence only, with **Replace** and **Clear**. If the last start ignored a saved
sign-in value, the page says so at the top and **Show me** opens its row. Credential
changes apply after the server restarts; the page shows the same **Restart now**
banner as **Server settings**, counting every pending change on the Home. Admins
see the rows as facts; only owners change them.

These credentials are the Home's; supplying them alone does not connect anyone's
company account.

> **Home company sign-in is not available yet.** AM-13 implementation and validation
> are incomplete. The following describes the approved target, not a shipped or
> usable company sign-in flow.

In **Identity providers**, an owner will be able to add a company Home sign-in
connection through WorkOS. The connection uses the same setup as a Team connection:
open the **Admin Portal**, choose the exact SSO connection, **Test sign-in**, then
turn it on. Test sign-in checks the connection without creating or linking an
account. If the platform credentials are missing, follow the link to **WorkOS**
in **Sign-in platforms** first.

Members see the company's name when signing in. A verified company domain can
route them to its exact connection; an unverified or ambiguous domain cannot
choose a connection automatically. New accounts can be created only when
**Policies** explicitly allows **Self-service**, that provider and the account
type. **Closed** and **Invitation only** do not become open signup merely because
SSO is enabled. Existing accounts can explicitly link the company identity;
matching an email address never links two accounts.

Turning the connection off stops new company sign-ins and keeps existing accounts
and linked identities. Removing it checks whether anyone would lose their only
usable way to sign in. SSO alone does not remove people who leave the company,
grant Home roles or add anyone to a Team. Each Team can still connect its own
company sign-in and directory from **Team → Authentication**; directory sync
remains Team-scoped. SMTP belongs on **Email**. **Automatic Team membership from sign-in** and **Approved GitHub
Enterprise hosts** are Home-level Team sign-in rules on this page.

## Reach and Email

**Reach** shows how devices reach the Home: its public and web-app addresses,
direct connections and host-side access method. It distinguishes saved addresses
from deployment-set and inferred addresses. Changing reachability does not change
the Home's authentication identity. Hosting controls appear only where an
executor exists.

**Email** configures SMTP, sender identity and a write-only password. Use **Save**
for staged changes, then **Send a test email** to check delivery. Readiness requires
both a mail transport and an address for links; the page points to **Reach** when
the link target is missing. If readiness or reachability cannot be read, the page
shows a failure with **Retry**, retaining useful last-known facts during refresh.

## Features and Data

**Features** shows server capabilities, with common choices first and advanced
families below. Unavailable rows explain their dependency, deployment lock or
build restriction. Device-only feature choices stay in the device's settings.

**Data** controls automatic deletion and per-domain retention, including the
**System records** disclosure. **Run a dry run** reports what would be deleted
without deleting it. Backups use the hosting runtime's existing controls where
available; the console does not add a hosted-server backup executor.

## Server settings and Runtime

**Server settings** covers configuration without a dedicated console page — sign-in
platform credentials, the Home's name and the Reach addresses live on their own
pages and are not repeated here. A row
shows its effective value and source. Live settings apply to later requests or
jobs; restart settings say **Applies after restart** and remain pending against
what the running server actually applied. Secrets never return their saved value.
Bootstrap settings are read-only and explain why they must be supplied before
startup or kept under deployment control.

Use the shared **Restart now** action when this device has a hosting-desktop,
Remote-host or connected-Machine executor. **Overview** offers the same action on
its *changes apply after restart* row, naming the settings that wait; on a device
that cannot restart the server, that row leads to **Runtime** instead. Acceptance of the task is not
completion: failures stay visible, and settings refresh after the runtime reports
that it is up. If approval is required, use the console's approval details; the
restart stays pending through the decision and task completion. A deployment-managed Home tells you to restart through your
deployment. **Runtime** also shows the version and available hosting operations.

**Discard** reverts saved pending restart changes to the running values and
records the change in **Activity**. If a saved setting prevents startup, set its
environment key explicitly to override it, then restart. Invalid saved values are
ignored at startup with a reason; an explicit environment value takes precedence.

**Activity** records administration changes with the actor. Secrets are recorded
as set or unset, never as their contents.

## When it goes wrong

- **You cannot administer this Home.** Your account holds no Home administration
  authority there. Roles are per Home; being an owner elsewhere means nothing here.
- **Home administration setup required.** The Home has no active owner yet. Print a
  one-time claim code on the machine running the server and paste it here, or, on the
  computer that hosts a Personal Home, choose **Make me the owner** — see
  [Home owner setup and recovery](/self-hosting/home-owner).
- **This Home does not offer Home administration.** It is running a version without it.
- **The Home is not answering.** You can keep reading the last known state; changes are
  unavailable until it reconnects. Happier labels this rather than showing stale data as
  current.
- **This change was not confirmed.** The request reached the Home but its answer was
  lost. It may have been applied — refresh and check before retrying.
